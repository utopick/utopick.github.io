Your exercises

Exercises you made in the warm-up editor (✎ on the warm-up bar). EXPORT saves them all in a .json file to share or keep; IMPORT adds the ones in a file. An exercise sung by a sample keeps the pad's number, not its sound: on another phone it uses that phone's pad, or the piano.

Note names

Letters or solfège, for every app: TRACK, TUNE and SING.Pocket.

Recording

Recordings are compressed (Opus or AAC, about 0.7 MB a minute). With BACKING on, a warm-up or a song take records the piano or the song together with your voice, like a performance; off records only you.

MIDI device (experimental)

A MIDI keyboard plays reference notes to sing to (SOUND turns them off, for keyboards with their own speakers); in SAMPLER it plays the selected pad at the key's pitch; in the exercise editor it writes or records the notes. ASSIGN ties pads, buttons and knobs to the sampler pads, START of the warm-up and the plane, and the next exercise. Chrome, Edge or Firefox; not Safari.

Pitch test

With OCTAVE-FREE on, singing a song an octave lower or higher than the original counts as in tune (most men singing a woman's song, and the other way round).

If the lyrics run early or late (a karaoke or live version, a long intro), move them here. AUTO lines the first line up with where the voice starts. Quickest: FIRST WORD · LYRICS (between KEY and SPEED), pressed the moment the singing starts; then − and + beside the lyrics for fine-tuning.

Or paste them

Tap the grid to place a note (tap it again to take it away): left to right is time, a column is half a beat, and a note lasts until the next one (the last one, two beats at most). R is the key the exercise starts from on each round. With a MIDI keyboard, the keys write at the cursor; ● MIDI records what you play in time (QUANTIZE snaps it to the grid). SOUND picks the piano or one of your sampler pads, retuned to every note.

Vocal reference

The pitch test compares your voice with a melody. By default the app finds that melody in the song itself, which works well for clear vocals and gets confused by loud guitars, synths or backing vocals.

If you have a vocal-only version of the same song (an a cappella or an isolated vocal track, with the same timing), pick it here: the app reads the melody from it instead. You still hear the full song; the vocal file is only used for scoring.
